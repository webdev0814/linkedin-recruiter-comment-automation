# Daily LinkedIn Comment Workflow

## Daily Limit

Start with 3 comments per day.

Do not exceed 5 comments per day without a separate manual review of account safety and comment quality.

## What To Look For

Prioritize posts from:

- recruiters hiring for AI, automation, analytics, business analysis, solution architecture, delivery, RTE, program, or public-sector technology roles
- hiring managers discussing agentic AI, AI implementation, workflow automation, governance, or operational transformation
- credible firms posting remote-friendly roles aligned to the target background
- public-sector, healthcare, civic tech, government contractor, Microsoft ecosystem, Salesforce, SAP, data, and analytics hiring conversations

Avoid posts that are:

- generic engagement bait
- political, combative, or culture-war oriented
- unrelated to the kind of work the user wants more of
- already overloaded with identical "interested" comments
- asking for low-fit onsite, travel-heavy, or junior roles

## Comment Formula

Use one sentence:

`Specific agreement or insight + practical business meaning + optional light signal of fit.`

Preferred tone:

- short enough to sound off-the-cuff
- natural enough that a real person might say it out loud
- willing to be lightly skeptical
- more human than polished
- okay to use a short add-on like `IMHO`, `tbh`, or a brief second sentence when it feels natural

Examples:

- "Automation works best when it saves time without making the process feel colder."
- "The human read still matters most here."
- "This is hard to do well."

## Daily Process

1. Collect 5 to 10 candidate post URLs manually.
2. Paste each URL, author, role/company, and post text or summary into the comment queue.
3. Score each post for role fit, brand fit, and account safety.
4. Draft one sentence per shortlisted post.
5. Pick the best 3 for the day.
6. Manually post only after reading the target post again inside LinkedIn.
7. Record whether the comment was posted, skipped, edited, or replied to.

## Quality Bar

A good comment should:

- be easy to read in under 5 seconds
- sound like a senior operator
- sound like a real person
- sound like a real operator, not a content writer
- add a real point, not just approval
- avoid "great post," "thanks for sharing," and "I'm interested" as the whole comment
- avoid direct selling unless the original post invites it
- keep the commenter visible for the right kind of work
- avoid abstract jargon like "workflow clarity," "operating model," or "structured signals" unless the post itself uses that language
- avoid sounding nicer than you actually feel about the topic

## Daily Prompt

Use this when you have candidate post links or pasted post text:

```text
Use the LinkedIn Recruiter Comment Prompt Contract.

Create a safe manual comment queue for these LinkedIn posts.

Rules:
- one sentence per comment
- short comments only
- no auto-posting
- no scraping
- prioritize recruiter and hiring relevance
- align with a practical brand: useful automation, trust, judgment, public-sector credibility, and operational clarity
- light emoji use is allowed, but use no more than one emoji in any comment and only when natural
- recommend no more than 3 comments to post today

For each post, return:
- post URL
- author/company
- relevance score from 1 to 5
- why it fits
- suggested one-sentence comment
- risk note
- post/skip recommendation
```
